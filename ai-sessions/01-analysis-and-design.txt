     - Assume: it opens on the 1st, uses balances as of the previous month-end

  3. What happens to reviews that are not finished by month-end? Do they carry
     over, or are they closed as missed?
     - Assume: they close as Incomplete and stay in the history. Next month's
       item for that facility shows "missed last month". A closed month is
       read-only.
  4. Who opens and closes a month? Can two months be open at the same time?
     - Assume: a manager does both by hand. Only one month is open at a time.
  5. Should the app send reminders (due soon, returned to you, cover starting)?
     - Assume: no email in v1. The home screen does that job.

  Roles and ownership

  6. [Top 5] There are 5 billers and 4 supervisors, which is close to one
     supervisor per biller. How is supervision organized? Do supervisors also
     own facilities? Is supervision by person, by group of facilities or by
     payer? Can a biller have two supervisors? Is a supervisor role vacant or
     about to change?
     - Assume: everyone reports to exactly one person. Supervisors can own
       facilities. A review goes to the person the owner reports to. Nobody
       approves their own work.
  7. Does exactly one biller own each facility, or is some work split by payer
     (for example Medicare and Medicaid)?
     - Assume: one owner per facility.
  8. [Top 5] Will anyone outside the RCM team use the app, for example facility

     - Assume: no. Only internal RCM staff use it.
  9. Are these the real team numbers or a simplified example?
     - Assume: the real team may be up to 10 times bigger. The model must still

  11. Does a cover take over the work or act on behalf of the owner? Does the

  12. Can one person's work be split between several covers?

  13. If a facility moves to another biller in the middle of a month, who

  14. Is time off recorded anywhere else, such as an HR system or a shared

  2. The monthly workflow

  Who does what

  - Day 1, open. A manager opens the month. The app creates one item per active
    facility. Each item is assigned to the facility's owner, is due on the 15th
    and links to last month's item. Supervisors check this month's absences and
    set covers.
  - Days 1 to 15, work. Billers do the review in the billing system and record
    it here: Start, Block with a reason, Submit with a summary, plus comments.
    Supervisors approve or return submissions, set covers and move single items.
    Managers watch the overview, chase blocked and overdue items and move
    facilities.
  - Day 16 to month-end, catch-up. Unfinished items are flagged Overdue. Work
    goes on with the same actions.
  - Month-end, close. A manager closes the month after a preview. Items not
    approved become Incomplete, and the month becomes read-only.
  - Next month. A facility's new item is flagged if it missed last month.

  Statuses

  ├─────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤

  ├─────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤

  - The owner is the item's assignee. The cover is whoever covers the owner
    today. The reviewer is the person the owner reports to. For an item a
    manager owns, another manager reviews.
  - Every action needs an open month and an item in the user's scope. Anyone who
    can see an item can comment on it.
  - A supervisor or manager who wants to do the work reassigns the item to
    themselves first. That keeps "who did the work" clear.
  - Self-approval example: a supervisor who covers their own biller can submit,
    but their manager then approves.

  Flags (computed, not statuses)

  - Overdue: the due date has passed and the item is not Approved.
  - Needs cover: the owner is away today and has no cover.
  - Covered by X: someone else is acting for the owner today.
  - Missed last month: last month's item ended Incomplete.

  An item can be overdue in any status, so an Overdue status would hide the real
  one.

  Reassignment

  Temporary cover
  - A supervisor (for their own team) or a manager records an absence: who is
    away, from when, until when, and who covers.
  - During those dates, the owner's open items appear on the cover's home screen
    tagged "Covering for X". The cover can do everything the owner can.
  - Ownership does not change. After the end date the items are back with the
    owner, so there is nothing to undo.
  - The history shows both names, for example "Submitted by B, covering for A".
  - Covers work for approvers too. A supervisor's cover can approve in their
    place.
  - Rules: the cover must be active and not away on those dates. Covers do not
    chain: if the cover is also away, the items show Needs cover. One person
    cannot have overlapping absences. An absence can end early. An absence can
    be saved without a cover, and its items show Needs cover until someone is
    picked.

  One item, this month only
  - A supervisor (own team) or a manager changes the owner of one open item. The
    facility's owner stays the same, so next month starts as normal. This is
    for splitting a long absence or balancing workload.

  Permanent move
  - A manager changes the facility's owner and gives a reason. The app ends the
    old assignment and starts a new one.
  - "Also move this month's open item" is on by default. Approved and Incomplete
    items keep their original owner, so the history stays accurate.
  - From the next month on, items go to the new owner.
  - A user who still owns facilities or open items cannot be deactivated.

  Month-end

  - Closing marks every item that is not Approved as Incomplete, including
    Submitted ones. The preview lists them, so the manager can ask supervisors
    to finish their reviews first.
  - The facility's next item links to the old one. The biller sees last month's
    summary and comments there.
  - A closed month is read-only. Work finished after the close does not count as
    done for that month.
  - Trade-off: we could keep unfinished items open across months instead. That
    keeps the work in one place, but then nobody can say what was done in a
    given month, and old items pile up. We give up late completion to keep each
    month's record clean.

  First screen per role

  - Biller, "My work": my items plus the ones I cover ("Covering for A until Nov
    21"). They are sorted by what needs me: Returned, Blocked, Overdue, Not
    started, In progress. Submitted and Approved items go below. Each row shows
    the facility, status, due date, flags, AR over 90, the last comment and the
    next action. A line on top reads like "4 items, 1 returned, due in 3 days".
  - Supervisor, "Team": items waiting for my review, oldest first, with Approve
    and Return. A "needs attention" list: blocked, overdue, needs cover, missed
    last month. One row per biller with open, submitted and approved counts, and
    who is away. My own items, if I own facilities.
  - Manager, "Overview": progress for the month across all facilities, with
    counts per status and days to the due date. Exceptions sorted by AR over 90.
    One row per team. A facility table with owner, reviewer, status, balances
    and flags. Actions for the month and for facilities.
  - Supervisors and managers can share one page with a different scope. That
    saves build time, and we give up a layout tuned to each role.

  3. First data model

  Entities

  - User: Id, Name, Email, Role (Biller, Supervisor, Manager), ReportsToId,
    IsActive.
  - Facility: Id, Code, Name, OwnerId, IsActive. An inactive facility gets no
    new items.
  - FacilityAssignment: Id, FacilityId, UserId, StartDate, EndDate (empty while
    current), Reason, ChangedById, ChangedAt.
  - ReviewCycle: Id, Year, Month, DueDate, Status (Open, Closed), OpenedById,
    OpenedAt, ClosedById, ClosedAt.
  - WorkItem (one facility, one month): Id, CycleId, FacilityId, AssigneeId,
    Status, DueDate, BlockedReason, Summary, SubmittedById, SubmittedAt,
    ApprovedById, ApprovedAt, PreviousItemId, ArTotal, ArOver90, Version.
  - WorkItemEvent (append-only): Id, WorkItemId, Type (Created, StatusChanged,
    Commented, Reassigned), FromStatus, ToStatus, FromUserId, ToUserId, ActorId,
    OnBehalfOfId, Text, CreatedAt.
  - Absence: Id, UserId, StartDate, EndDate, CoverUserId (optional), Note,
    CreatedById, CreatedAt.

  Relationships

  - Each User reports to another User through ReportsToId, forming a tree:
    biller, then supervisor, then manager.
  - A Facility has one current owner and many FacilityAssignment rows.
  - A ReviewCycle has many WorkItems, one per facility (a unique key on CycleId
    and FacilityId).
  - A WorkItem has one assignee and many events. It links to last month's item
    for the same facility.
  - An Absence links the user who is away and the optional cover.

  Decisions and what we give up

  - The reviewer is not stored. It comes from the owner's reporting line at the
    moment someone acts, so pending reviews follow a change in reporting. We
    give up a record of who the reviewer was meant to be. Who actually approved
    is in the events.
  - Facility.OwnerId plus FacilityAssignment give cheap reads of the current
    owner and a full history. We give up a single source of truth, so one
    handler writes both in one transaction.
  - Balances live on WorkItem because they have the same grain (facility and
    month). We give up keeping several snapshots per month.
  - Comments are events. That gives one timeline per item in one table. We give
    up a strict table per event type.
  - Version is an optimistic concurrency token. If an owner and a cover act at
    the same time, the second one gets a conflict instead of silently
    overwriting the first.
  - Times: timestamps are stored in UTC. Due dates and absences are plain dates
    in one business time zone.
  - Two kinds of record: the status fields on WorkItem hold the current state,
    and the events are the permanent record.

  History we keep

  - Every status change, comment and reassignment, with who did it and on whose
    behalf. These are never edited or deleted.
  - Facility ownership over time.
  - Closed months and their items, read-only.
  - Absences and who covered them.
  - Not kept in the prototype: who viewed what.

  Why no patient-level data

  - One line of account-level AR (resident, account number, payer, dates of
    service, balance) is PHI under HIPAA. Account numbers are one of the 18
    identifiers in the HIPAA Safe Harbor rule, so a list without names is still
    PHI.
  - PHI comes with duties a prototype cannot meet (access logging, encryption at
    rest, hosting rules) and with breach risk. This repo is public.
  - The problems in the brief are at facility level: owner, status, cover and
    month-end. Account detail already lives in the billing system, so we point
    to it instead of copying it.
  - What we give up: the app cannot show which accounts inside a facility are
    stuck. That is the likely next step, and it would make this a PHI system.
  - Comments are where PHI could leak in. The comment box says "no resident
    names or account numbers".

  4. Options for the first version

  Option A: monthly worklist

  - What a user can do: sign in as any seeded user and see a home screen for
    their role. Make every status transition, with its rules. Record absences
    with a cover. Reassign a single item. Move a facility permanently. Open and
    close a month with a preview. See each item's history.
  - Leaves out: balance import, checklist, notifications, exports, admin screens
    (users, facilities and reporting lines come from seed data) and
    account-level data.
  - Main risk: if "done" means more than a sign-off, this is only a status
    tracker. "Needs attention" is driven by dates and status alone.
  - Build: about 75 minutes, with tests.

  Option B: A plus a review checklist

  - Adds: a fixed list of steps per review. Examples: aging over 90 reviewed,
    Medicaid pending list updated, credit balances checked, AR call held with
    the facility. Submit needs the required steps ticked, and progress shows on
    every screen.
  - Leaves out: the same as A. The steps come from seed data and there is no
    editor.
  - Main risk: the steps are our guess. If they are wrong, people tick them
    without reading, and they stop trusting the app.
  - Build: about 95 minutes.

  Option C: A plus a balance import

  - Adds: CSV upload of month-end balances per facility and payer group.
    Attention is ranked by money: AR over 90 and the change from last month.
  - Leaves out: the checklist and the real export mapping.
  - Main risk: we guess the file format, and validation eats the time. Numbers
    that look real lead to questions we cannot answer.
  - Build: about 120 minutes.

  Not offered: an account-level worklist. It needs PHI and a real data feed. It
  is the likely direction after v1.

  My pick

  I would pick A, plus two seeded numbers per item (total AR and AR over 90),
  with no import. A covers all four problems the brief names: what needs
  attention, who owns what, what is done, and how to move work when someone is
  out. It fits in the session with tests. B and C depend on answers we do not
  have yet (the real steps and the real export format). The two numbers take
  minutes to add, let the overview sort by money and show where an import would
  fit later. If session 2 runs long, cut reopen first, then single-item
  reassignment.

  Running locally (same for all options)

  - The database is a SQLite file, created and seeded on first start. No SQL
    Server and no Docker.
  - Sign-in is a picker of seeded users. The API accepts the demo user header
    only in Development.
  - Two commands: dotnet run for the API and npm start for the Angular app. The
    Angular dev server forwards API calls, so there is no CORS setup. You need
    the .NET 10 SDK and Node.js LTS.
  - What we give up: SQLite is not SQL Server, so decimal sorting, concurrency
    tokens and collation behave differently. The EF model avoids
    database-specific features, and switching to SQL Server is a config change.
    SQL Server in Docker would match production but needs Docker and a large
    download. LocalDB only runs on Windows and is a SQL Server install anyway.
